1. The plan he was sent to evaluate is not the plan the page sells.
“Starter at 3 activations and Pro at 10 are both dead on arrival for multi-client agency work. The $149 'Unlimited' card — tagged Scale Ready — is the one, but its feature list runs below the fold, so I'm scrolling to see everything it includes.”
Karim arrived with a brief to price the 'Scale' plan. The pricing page's top tier is called 'Unlimited', with 'Scale Ready' as a tag on the card. He worked it out in a step, but he had to work it out — and he is the kind of buyer who arrives with a plan name from a colleague, a deck or a sales email.
Why this costs trust
Naming drift between what your team says and what the page shows costs nothing when the buyer is patient and costs the deal when they are comparing three vendors in one afternoon.
Actor friction note · Step 5 · verbatim
The plan I came to evaluate is named 'Unlimited' while being tagged 'Scale Ready' — I initially looked for a 'Scale' plan label, so the naming creates a small moment of doubt about whether a separate higher tier exists.
What to restore
Pick one name for the top tier and use it everywhere, or make 'Scale' an explicit alias on the card so a search for it lands somewhere.
Evidence boundary: One evaluator's read of the public pricing page on this date; the brief he was given is our construction, but it mirrors how buyers arrive.
