Where Haildesk lost Anushka

CRITICALSteps 34–41
1. The AI Responder switch is invisible to everything except a mouse.
“The AI Responder switch still looks off — I need it on and saved before any test conversation will use my FAQ. Flipping it on and hitting Save AI settings this time, then I'm going straight to Conversations to actually try it.”
Anushka spent the last third of her session trying to switch the AI Responder on, and twice believed she had. A hands-on inspection of the live page afterwards explains why nothing happened: the switch is a visually hidden checkbox behind a decorative track, with no label bound to it, so its accessible name resolves to the bare word 'on'. The only properly labelled control in that panel is the neighbouring 'Limit AI to active hours' checkbox — named activeSchedule.enabled underneath — and that is what both attempts resolved to. The responder stayed off through the green 'Saved!' that followed.
Why this costs trust
This is the switch her whole task hinged on. A screen-reader user hears 'on, checkbox' with no idea what it governs; keyboard and assistive users, and any agent acting for them, cannot reliably tell the responder from the schedule limiter — and the save confirmation reports success either way. Silent misconfiguration of the core feature is the most expensive kind.
Actor friction note · Step 41 · verbatim
The save button for AI Settings is pushed offscreen below the fold with no sticky footer — I've already toggled this once without a clear confirmation, which is why I second-guess whether the setting actually stuck.
What to restore
Give the switch a real accessible name (a bound label or aria-label) instead of the inherited 'on', name the underlying controls for what they govern, and reflect the responder's live state ('AI Responder is OFF') in the save confirmation.
Evidence boundary: The mis-clicks were made by our synthetic user; dispatch receipts prove which control took them, and a later hands-on inspection of the live settings page confirmed the missing accessible name. A sighted mouse user aiming at the visible track will hit the right switch — the defect is that every other route to it is ambiguous.
