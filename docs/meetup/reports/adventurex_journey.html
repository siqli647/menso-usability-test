Final Verdict

Completed the task
Yuki was able to confirm the next edition details from the homepage hero
• The biggest usability issue was that, across repeated scrolling from hero to footer, they never found a visible registration or application entry, only marketing content, sponsor logos, and photo carousels.
• For a fast-scanning student applicant, this means the homepage does not support the core job-to-be-done of quickly deciding whether and how to apply, increasing abandonment risk even when interest is high.
• Add a persistent primary “Apply” / “报名” CTA in the header and hero, and pair it with an explicit application-status label so users can immediately tell whether applications are open, closed, or not yet available.
Final Confidence
0.85

UX/UI Frictions

1. As an impatient, mobile-native student scanning for a fast path to apply, I expect a hackathon homepage to expose a…
Add a persistent primary CTA labeled “Apply” / “报名” in the header and hero section of the homepage, and repeat it near the footer so students can enter the application workflow without scanning recap content.
2. I scan first for date, location, and whether applications are live.
On the homepage hero, separate event schedule from application status and replace the contradictory status with a clear state label such as “Applications closed,” “Applications open,” or “Next edition details coming soon” next to the date/location line.
3. Before I even find eligibility or signup, the page signals low reliability through placeholder-looking stats.
Replace zero-value stat cards on the homepage with verified current numbers, or hide the stats module entirely until real values are available so the first-scroll experience does not look broken.
