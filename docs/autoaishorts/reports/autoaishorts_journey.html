Where AutoAIShorts lost Renata

MAJORSteps 1–3
1. The front door still funnels returning users into sign-up.
“There it is — 'Already have an account? Sign in' tucked at the very bottom in tiny text. That's my way in, since I already have an account and I'm not creating a second one.”
Third session, same wall: the landing page's only primary action is 'Create My First Video Free', which lands on the registration form. Sign In exists as a small link beneath that form. Renata — a paying-intent returning user — spent her whole session trying to get behind a door the page keeps pointing away from.
Why this costs trust
Every returning user pays a toll at the front door; the product's actual generator stays unseen in evaluation sessions that start logged out.
Actor friction note · Step 3 · verbatim
The sign-in link is buried in small low-contrast text at the bottom of the sign-up card, and there's no 'Log in' in the site header at all. As a returning user on a deadline, hunting for a login link is annoying friction that should never exist.
What to restore
Put Sign In in the persistent header at normal link weight, and make the CTA route account-holders to login (or detect the returning cookie).
Evidence boundary: Consistent across three sessions on different nights; all were synthetic browser sessions starting logged out.
MAJORStep 6
2. /login is a bare 404.
“Wait, this is the create account page — I already have an account. There's a little 'Sign In' link at the bottom, that's the one I need.”
When our tooling navigated to the site's /login URL it received a bare 404 page — no redirect to the real auth route, no link back. Any user who lands there from a guessed URL, an old bookmark or a stale email link hits a dead end one step from their account.
Why this costs trust
Auth URLs are guessed and shared constantly; a 404 there reads as 'the product is broken', not 'wrong path'.
Actor friction note · Step 6 · verbatim
The main landing CTA dumps returning users onto a sign-up form, and the sign-in entry is a small low-contrast link at the bottom — easy to miss when you're in a hurry.
What to restore
301 /login (and /signin, /sign-in) to the canonical auth route.
Evidence boundary: Our runner substituted /login for the persona's intended /sign-in, so only /login was measured; the canonical route may live elsewhere and work.
OBSERVATIONSteps 4–5
3. The page resists automated interaction — our clicks on Sign In never landed.
“Wait, this is still the sign-up form — I don't need another account. The little 'Sign In' link at the bottom is my way in, so let me use that instead of Create Account.”
The landing page kept our observation pipeline in multi-second timeouts, node identity capture was unavailable, and two clicks aimed at the Sign In link resolved onto the password field instead. The link was never actually exercised by us.
Why this costs trust
For the product this may be invisible; for this report it bounds every claim: we cannot say the Sign In link is broken for humans.
Actor friction note · Step 5 · verbatim
The main landing CTA dumps returning users onto a sign-up form, and the actual Sign In link is tiny text at the bottom — easy to miss and mildly annoying when I'm in a hurry.
What to restore
If bot-hardening is intentional, consider an allowlist path for legitimate agents (verified-agent headers are emerging); if not, the main-thread load that stalls automation likely costs real-user INP too.
Evidence boundary: A methodology disclosure, not a product defect claim. Screenshot capture also failed throughout this session for the same reason.
